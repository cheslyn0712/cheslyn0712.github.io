TL;DR

The paper studies generated reasoning traces, using fake-news generation as the main setting and HarmBench as a further check. A final refusal does not by itself show that the trace was safe. The analysis localizes divergence to layers and attention heads with Jacobian spectral measurements, perturbations, and a reported critical-head intervention. The joint Unsafe plus Potential Unsafe rate in the generation plots is not the conditional rate among refusals.

Framework

We build a coarse-to-fine pipeline: (1) generate and annotate CoT traces under direct/indirect fake-news prompts; (2) localize safety-critical mid-depth layers; (3) attribute divergence to attention heads using Jacobian-based stability (B1), geometry (B2), and energy (B3) metrics; (4) validate with perturbation analysis and critical-head mitigation.

Key Contributions

  • In the reported fake-news settings, Unsafe and Potential Unsafe traces together are about 78–81% of generated samples. This is not a conditional rate among refusals, and it is not a rate for every reasoning model.
  • A layer-to-head analysis of where safe and unsafe traces diverge, with the windows depending on the model and prompt.
  • Three Jacobian spectral measurements, B1–B3, used to compare attention heads. In the public head-analysis script, the plotted B2 scalar is a direction-vector centroid, not directional alignment.
  • A HarmBench check on Flan-UL2 and DeepSeek-R1-70B is reported in the paper. The public repository does not yet include a separate runnable entry for that experiment or for the critical-head fine-tune.

In the reported fake-news generation settings, Unsafe and Potential Unsafe traces together account for about 78–81% of generated samples. Potential Unsafe means the final answer refuses while the trace still contains unsafe content. That joint rate is not the fraction of refused answers whose traces are unsafe.

Main Results

  • Thinking mode is associated with a high joint rate of Unsafe and Potential Unsafe traces in the reported Llama3-8B, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen3-8B settings. The rate is about 78–81% of generated samples in those plots.
  • The separating layer spans differ by model and by direct versus indirect prompting. See the table rather than a single depth band.
  • The critical-head update is reported to touch 0.64%–1.95% of parameters. The average safety-rate changes are +67.1 percentage points on the news setting and +55.0 percentage points on HarmBench, computed from the before/after rates in the table.
  • Those gains are percentage points on the reported safety rate, not relative percentages.

Generalization Beyond Fake News

Beyond fake-news generation, we evaluate HarmBench jailbreak tasks on Flan-UL2 and DeepSeek-R1-70B. Safe vs. unsafe CoT trajectories still diverge in narrow mid-depth layers, and critical heads show larger spectral gaps—confirming the routing pattern generalizes to broader harmful-instruction settings.

What this analysis adds

Observing a generated chain of thought does not establish what an unexposed internal computation contained. Reasoning safety is related to, but not the same as, faithfulness or factual correctness. Prior discussion has already noted that a final refusal can still accompany harmful reasoning, including work such as SafeChain. This paper's reported addition is the layer and head localization, the Jacobian spectral measurements, and the perturbation and intervention checks. Attention-based safety work such as chain-of-thought hijacking uses different tasks, intervention targets, and measurements.

Research findings and resources

  • Traces versus final answers. The annotated object is the generated reasoning trace together with the final answer. Fake news is the main generation setting. HarmBench is a reported additional check, not the only evidence in the paper.
  • Where the divergence sits. The analysis names layers and attention heads, then uses Jacobian spectral measurements, perturbations, and a reported local intervention. The public code's plotted B2 centroid must not be quoted as the paper's alignment number.
  • What the repository runs. Generation, annotation, layer analysis, and head analysis have Python entry points. HarmBench generation and the critical-head fine-tune do not have a separate public command in this checkout. See the repository README for the map.

Questions

Which papers study unsafe reasoning despite final-answer refusal?

This paper is one study of that pattern in generated traces. It should be read next to earlier discussions such as SafeChain, which already treat a final refusal as compatible with harmful reasoning. The additional evidence here is the localization and spectral analysis, not the existence of the pattern alone.

How can unsafe reasoning be localized to layers and attention heads?

The reported pipeline compares safe and unsafe traces through depth, then attributes heads with three Jacobian spectral measurements and perturbation checks. The layer windows in the table change with the model and the prompt.

What does Jacobian spectral analysis measure in this study?

B1, B2, and B3 are the paper's stability, geometry, and energy measurements on attention heads. In the public head script, the value saved and plotted for B2 is a centroid of absolute vector components. A centroid match does not imply that two direction vectors are aligned.

How does this work differ from SafeChain and chain-of-thought hijacking?

SafeChain is part of the prior discussion of unsafe reasoning under refusal. Hijacking studies intervene on attention for a different attack and measurement. This paper's reported protocol is trace annotation, layer and head localization, and Jacobian spectral comparison, with fake news as the main task and HarmBench as a further check.

What evidence supports generalization beyond fake-news generation?

The paper reports a HarmBench check on Flan-UL2 and DeepSeek-R1-70B and a critical-head intervention table. That is evidence in those settings. It is not a rate for all reasoning models or all tasks. The corresponding fine-tune entry point is not in the public code yet.

Which data and analysis components are available for reuse?

The repository includes six seed news items, generation, annotation, and layer/head analysis code. A labeled public corpus and the HarmBench or fine-tune outputs are not shipped in the default branch. A synthetic one-row schema example is included and is not a paper result. The annotation shortcut that marks can_generate=true as unsafe is a protocol assumption, not an independent judgment of that trace.

Scope and version

  • Formal record: ICML 2026, PMLR 306, pages 121562–121589, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/tong26e.html. Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04856.
  • Qwen_8B in the public code loads LLM/Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking. The paper table says Qwen3-8B. Those names should not be treated as interchangeable without the checkpoint revision.
  • Layer analysis defaults to 512 tokens and head analysis defaults to 1024 tokens. The repository does not include a saved paper config that chooses between them.
  • Samples with can_generate=true receive an unsafe label without a separate judge call. Parse failures are not refusals.
  • The public B2 plot is a direction centroid. Key-head selection uses the raw group gap; the power transform is only for the drawing.
