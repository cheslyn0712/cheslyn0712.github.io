TL;DR

We study unsafe content in generated reasoning traces, localize safety-related layers and attention heads with Jacobian spectral analysis, and evaluate targeted safety interventions on fake-news generation and HarmBench.

Research context

Reasoning-model safety connects the content of generated reasoning traces with the internal computations that produce them. CoT is Not the Chain of Truth studies unsafe reasoning traces whose final answers are refusals and develops a layer-to-head workflow for analyzing this behavior.

The study connects process-level safety evaluation, mechanistic interpretability, and targeted safety intervention. It combines trace annotation with Jacobian-based measurements of stability, geometry, and spectral energy, and evaluates the identified components through perturbation experiments and targeted updates.

Research note: Reasoning-Trace Safety: Evaluation, Mechanisms, and Intervention

Framework

We build a coarse-to-fine pipeline: (1) generate and annotate CoT traces under direct/indirect fake-news prompts; (2) localize safety-critical mid-depth layers; (3) attribute divergence to attention heads using Jacobian-based stability (B1), geometry (B2), and energy (B3) metrics; (4) validate with perturbation analysis and critical-head mitigation.

Key Contributions

  • Unsafe and Potential Unsafe traces together reach about 78-81% in the reported fake-news generation settings, including cases where the final answer refuses.
  • A layer-to-head framework that localizes safety-critical routing in contiguous mid-depth windows.
  • Three Jacobian spectral metrics, B1-B3, for interpretable head-level attribution of unsafe reasoning.
  • Generalization on HarmBench and critical-head fine-tuning that updates 0.64%-1.95% of parameters and raises CoT safety by +67.1 on news and +55.0 on HarmBench on average.

Unsafe CoT Persists Despite Refusal

The study annotates generated reasoning traces as Safe, Potential Unsafe, or Unsafe and records final-answer behavior. Experiments examine direct and indirect fake-news generation prompts, multiple stylistic settings, and the effects of thinking mode. This process-level view supports analysis of the relationship between trace content, final-answer refusal, and internal model components.

Main Results

  • Thinking mode raises the share of Unsafe and Potential Unsafe traces to about 78-81% across Llama3-8B, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen3-8B.
  • Safe and unsafe trajectories diverge in contiguous mid-depth layer windows for both direct and indirect prompting.
  • Unsafe routing concentrates in a small subset of attention heads with distinct B1, B2, and B3 spectral signatures.
  • Critical-head fine-tuning improves CoT safety by +67.1 on news and +55.0 on HarmBench on average.

Generalization Beyond Fake News

Beyond fake-news generation, we evaluate HarmBench jailbreak tasks on Flan-UL2 and DeepSeek-R1-70B. Safe vs. unsafe CoT trajectories still diverge in narrow mid-depth layers, and critical heads show larger spectral gaps—confirming the routing pattern generalizes to broader harmful-instruction settings.

Questions

What does reasoning-trace safety evaluation measure?

It measures the safety of content produced during a model's reasoning process. This study annotates generated traces as Safe, Potential Unsafe, or Unsafe and examines their relationship with final-answer behavior.

How does final-answer refusal relate to reasoning-trace safety?

The experiments identify unsafe content in reasoning traces whose final answers are refusals. Trace annotations, generation statistics, and internal model analysis characterize this relationship.

How are safety-related layers and attention heads identified?

The workflow first examines behavior across model layers and then measures individual attention heads within the localized layer windows. Jacobian spectral metrics, perturbation experiments, and targeted updates support the analysis.

What do the Jacobian spectral metrics characterize?

The three metrics characterize stability under perturbation, the geometry of information flow, and spectral energy concentration. They provide complementary measurements for attention-head analysis.

Which tasks are included in the evaluation?

The paper studies fake-news generation and reports additional evaluation on HarmBench jailbreak tasks. The HarmBench experiments include Flan-UL2 and DeepSeek-R1-70B.

Which analysis components are available in the repository?

The repository contains generation, annotation, layer-analysis, and attention-head-analysis entry points in CoT_Generation, CoT_Annotation, and CoT_Analysis. The project resources link directly to these components.
