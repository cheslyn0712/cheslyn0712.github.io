TL;DR

LLM-generated comments can attack comment-based fake news detectors through fact distortion, logical confusion, and emotional manipulation. CogniDir scores each attack group's vulnerability and reallocates the next epoch's comment mixture with the deterministic Dirichlet mean. On the reported Weibo20 robust-training comparison, Macro-F1 moves from 0.803 (ARG) to 0.947, a relative gain of about 17.9% over that baseline.

Cognitive Attacks Break Static Defenses

On Weibo16, the paper's Figure 1 shows a previous comment-aware detector losing more than 18% F1 under different malicious-comment types, while CogniDir stays near 0.94 F1 across the displayed categories. The 18% figure is that reported drop, not a claim about every detector.

Framework

After each epoch, CogniDir computes a vulnerability score for fact distortion, logical confusion, and emotional manipulation. It sets alpha_j = s_j + 1 and allocates the next comment budget with the Dirichlet mean p_j = alpha_j / sum_k alpha_k. That mean is a deterministic mixture. The paper's Appendix H also writes it as the unique solution of an entropy-regularized allocation, which is not an unconditional robustness guarantee.

Key Contributions

  • A mechanism-labeled synthesis setup for fact distortion, logical confusion, and emotional manipulation. These names describe how the attacks are constructed in the paper, not a separate human-subject causal study.
  • InfoDirichlet Resampling: vulnerability scores are mapped to the next training mixture by the deterministic Dirichlet mean.
  • Reported robust-detection comparisons on Weibo16, Weibo20, and RumourEval-19, including the attack-success definition above.

Cognitive Attack Taxonomy

Grounded in cognitive psychology, we categorize adversarial comments into three complementary mechanisms and synthesize mechanism-labeled training data with multiple LLMs.

FACT

Fact Distortion

Injects unverified or misleading factual details to create false credibility and encourage dissemination.

LOGIC

Logical Confusion

Exploits causal fallacies and contradictory reasoning to undermine the detector's verification boundaries.

EMOTION

Emotional Manipulation

Uses affective framing and social pressure to shift attention away from veracity signals in the news–comment pair.

Main Results

  • On Weibo16, the paper's Figure 1 reports a previous method dropping by more than 18% F1 under malicious comments, with CogniDir near 0.94 F1 across the displayed attack categories.
  • In the robust-training column, the relative gain over the second-best reported baseline is +9.0% on Weibo16 (0.867 GenFEND to 0.945), +17.9% on Weibo20 (0.803 ARG to 0.947), and +14.5% on RumourEval-19 (0.709 L-Defense to 0.812). The Weibo20 change is 0.144 absolute Macro-F1 and about 17.9% relative to 0.803.
  • Mean ASR after adversarial group training is 0.116, a 45% relative reduction versus the reported untrained setting. ASR counts flips among originally correct predictions.
  • The ablation removes malicious-comment generation, the vulnerability score, or Dirichlet expectation allocation. Those are component removals on the reported tables, not a proof that every component is necessary outside these datasets.

Attack Success Rate (ASR)

Attack success rate is the fraction of originally correct predictions that become incorrect after malicious comments are added. The denominator is the set of originally correct predictions. The paper reports that, after adversarial group training, mean ASR falls to 0.116, a 45% reduction relative to the setting without that training.

Adaptive Robustness During Training

Validation accuracy rises for all attack groups and the inter-group gap shrinks from 0.23 to 0.08, showing IDR reallocates learning toward weaker mechanisms.

Allocation

The allocation uses the deterministic Dirichlet mean. It does not sample a Dirichlet posterior, and it is not a Bayesian prior over detector parameters. Appendix H says the allocation properties do not imply a globally optimal training-data mixture.

Research findings and resources

  • Mechanism-specific comments. The paper constructs and evaluates fact distortion, logical confusion, and emotional manipulation as separate comment attacks. Cite it for that attack taxonomy and the reported group-wise gaps, not for an independent psychology experiment.
  • Feedback-driven mixture. The training allocation is the deterministic Dirichlet mean of Appendix H. The entropy-regularized view explains that mean. It does not add an unconditional global robustness guarantee.
  • What is reusable now. The code repository is https://github.com/cheslyn0712/CogniDir. A standalone evaluator that scores an outside detector without training CogniDir is not yet a documented public entry point.

Questions

How do LLM-generated adversarial comments affect fake news detectors?

In the reported experiments, comments generated under fact distortion, logical confusion, and emotional manipulation change predictions of comment-aware detectors. Figure 1 on Weibo16 shows a previous method losing more than 18% F1. The effect size is for those datasets and attacks, not for every detector.

Why can vulnerability differ across manipulation mechanisms?

The three groups are different comment constructions. The paper measures different attack success and validation accuracy across them, then feeds that difference back into the next mixture. The gap is an empirical property of these models and datasets.

How does CogniDir adapt the adversarial training mixture?

After an epoch it scores each group, sets alpha_j = s_j + 1, and uses the Dirichlet mean p_j = alpha_j / sum alpha as the next sampling proportion. Training follows that mean. It does not draw a new Dirichlet sample for the allocation.

How does it differ from MALCOM, SINCon, and comment augmentation?

MALCOM establishes malicious-comment attacks. SINCon uniformizes node influence in a message propagation tree. Generated-comment detectors such as GenFEND add comments to help classification. GSFND adjusts the real/fake class ratio. CogniDir adjusts how much training exposure each attack mechanism receives.

Which datasets, languages, and attack settings were evaluated?

The reported benchmark tables use Weibo16 and Weibo20, which are Chinese, and RumourEval-19, which is English. Each dataset is evaluated in its own split. That is not the same as a train-on-one-language, test-on-another transfer protocol.

Does the allocation use a deterministic Dirichlet mean, and what does its theory establish?

Yes. Equation 8 and Appendix H use the Dirichlet mean. Appendix H.3 says the listed properties do not imply a globally optimal training mixture. The ACL HTML abstract that mentions a Dirichlet prior and a guarantee of the optimal mixture does not match this PDF; the PDF wording is the one used here.
